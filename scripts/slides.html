The problem

Paper logs, wet gloves, one misheard digit

  • Every US food establishment is required to log temperatures — most of it is still clipboards, checked with wet, gloved hands, hours after the fact.
  • A 38°F reading logged as 48°F isn't a UX nitpick — it's a real food-safety failure, and it's invisible until an inspector (or an outbreak) finds it.
  • The fix has to be hands-free (cooks are working) and has to catch the misheard digit in the same breath, not at the next audit.

The idea

Say it once. Hear it read back. It's logged.

  • A cook calls out a reading mid-task — "walk-in cooler, thirty-eight," "chicken breast, one fifty-two."
  • TempCheck logs it, checks it against real FDA limits, and speaks the exact number back before saving anything flagged.
  • A cook can self-correct mid-sentence ("steam table, one twenty — no wait, one forty") and TempCheck keeps the corrected number, not the first one.
  • Out-of-range readings get a spoken corrective action, not a silent red dot nobody sees until the shift ends.

Why the rule engine is separate from the LLM

The safety verdict is never a language model's guess

  • The model's only job is extraction: location/item, number, unit. A plain, unit-tested comparison against FDA Food Code limits decides safe / amber / red.
  • Cold holding ≤41°F · hot holding ≥135°F · poultry ≥165°F · ground/injected meat ≥155°F · whole muscle ≥145°F · fish/seafood/eggs ≥145°F · reheating ≥165°F.
  • Plus a two-stage cooling curve (135→70°F within 2h, then →41°F within 6h, FDA 3-501.14(A)), pairing a spoken "cooling start" with a later "cooling check."
  • 39 automated tests cover every category, every boundary, unit conversion, and implausible readings — zero hallucination risk in the verdict itself.

Business value & originality

Every kitchen already has to do this — almost nobody else is building it

Business value

  • Temperature logging is a health-code requirement, not optional, at every US food establishment.
  • Turns a paper chore into a real-time, hands-free, provably-checked habit — the exported PDF is already in the format an inspector expects.
  • No new hardware: runs in a browser tab a kitchen already has open.

Originality

  • Most of the gallery is general-purpose copilots, dispatch/fraud agents, or interview coaches.
  • TempCheck is deliberately narrow: one real workflow, done with enough rigor — deterministic rules, mandatory readback, measured accuracy, real end-to-end verification, a judge-friendly demo mode — that it could plausibly go into a real kitchen tomorrow.

Known limitations, stated plainly

What isn't solved yet

  • The accuracy test set is synthesized speech and noise, not real kitchen recordings — a disclosed, reproducible proxy, not a certified real-world number.
  • Transient, speech-like noise (a fryer, a shouting coworker) is measurably harder for the STT than steady noise — 52.8%/47.2% vs. 88–100%.
  • Cooling-curve tracking pairs a start and one later check and assumes temperature only decreased in between — it doesn't independently confirm an intermediate point, and the pending state doesn't survive a reload.
  • Shift storage is local to one browser (localStorage) — no multi-device or multi-user backend yet.
